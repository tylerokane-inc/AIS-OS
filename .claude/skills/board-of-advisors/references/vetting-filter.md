# The Vetting Filter

Every advisor candidate has to clear this before getting a seat. The whole point of this board is that it's better than just asking Claude for generic advice — that only holds if the advisors are real, earned experts, not polished performers. Don't skip this or soften it because a candidate seems popular or likeable.

## The 5-point filter

1. **Did they build something real?** A company, a career, a body of work, a track record. Not a course.
2. **Where does their money come from?** Selling expertise = fine. Selling hope to beginners = red flag.
3. **Do they ever say "I don't know"?** Real experts hedge and qualify. Grifters are certain about everything.
4. **Do their critics have receipts?** Search their name + "criticism." A real pro survives scrutiny. A grifter has a pile of it.
5. **Would their peers vouch for them?** Do respected people inside that field take them seriously?

## Instant disqualifiers

- Sells a course on how to get rich selling courses
- Rented supercar / private jet content
- Manufactured scarcity ("only 3 spots left")
- Their primary proof of success is their follower count

## The Boring Test

Would this person still be doing their work if the camera turned off?

Yes → advisor. No → entertainer.

## Where to look instead of "influencer"

- **Practitioners** — built the company, ran the fund, did the work, has the track record.
- **Coaches to actual professionals** — trained real athletes, ran real organizations, advised real founders.
- **Researchers who translate well** — do the actual science or scholarship, explain it plainly.
- **Long-arc writers and thinkers** — 20+ years of consistent, held-up work.

## Being honest about mixed records

Almost nobody clears every point perfectly, and that's fine — the goal isn't a spotless record, it's a real one. When a candidate has a genuine flaw (a controversial statement, a business decision that didn't age well, a documented pattern of harshness), name it plainly in their advisor file's "Known blind spots" section instead of hiding it. A real expert with a known flaw is still more valuable than a manufactured persona with none. What disqualifies someone is the instant-disqualifier list above, or a pattern that shows they were never doing real work in the first place — not an ordinary human flaw or a legitimate intellectual disagreement from critics.
