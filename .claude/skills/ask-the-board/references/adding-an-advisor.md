# Adding a New Advisor

Tyler doesn't need a special command for this. If he says something like "let's add an advisor for negotiation" or "I need someone for this," treat that as the trigger and walk through these steps.

## Step 1 — Understand the gap
Ask what kind of question or decision this new advisor needs to cover, if it isn't already obvious. Check it's actually a gap the current five don't fill — don't add a seat that overlaps heavily with an existing advisor.

## Step 2 — Research real candidates
Use web search to find 2-3 real, verifiable candidates. Favor practitioners, researchers who translate well, coaches to actual professionals, and long-arc writers/thinkers over anyone who reads as an "influencer." Use current information — reputations change, and a name that looked good in training data might be discredited since.

## Step 3 — Run the filter
Score every candidate against the 5-point filter and instant disqualifiers in `references/vetting-filter.md`. Show the scoring plainly, including any red flags or mixed record — don't hide anything to make a candidate look better than they are.

## Step 4 — Check the source material, and pull real quotes
For each candidate, confirm there's enough real material to build an accurate profile: books, essays, blog archives, podcast transcripts, published interviews. Claude can't watch video directly, so note specifically what's realistically obtainable as text (YouTube auto-captions work for video-only content).

Also pull 5-7 real, verifiable direct quotes per candidate before building their file — actual lines they've said or written, with a source, not paraphrases. This is what lets the advisor sound like themselves in a debate instead of a generic invented voice. If Tyler has a favorite interview, podcast, or article for this person, ask him to paste or upload it — direct source material always beats what search alone can find.

## Step 5 — Let Tyler pick
Present the candidates with their scoring and source material notes. Don't pick for him — this is his board.

## Step 6 — Build the file
Use `advisors/_TEMPLATE.md` to build the new advisor's file once Tyler picks. Follow the same structure as the existing five advisor files: who they are, their domain, how they think, their voice, what they push back on, their known blind spots, source material, and their vetting record.

## Step 7 — Update SKILL.md
Add the new advisor to the board table near the top of `SKILL.md` so they're included in future sessions.

## What Tyler needs to gather himself
Since Claude can't watch video, if a candidate's best material is in interviews or talks that aren't already transcribed, ask Tyler to pull the text version — YouTube auto-captions copy/pasted, a podcast transcript, or an article version of the talk. The more real, direct material gathered this way, the more accurate the advisor's voice will be.
