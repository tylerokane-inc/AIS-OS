## 2026-07-16
**Question:** Business partner wants to run an intentionally shocking/offensive marketing campaign because the data shows outrage-bait gets far more engagement and sales than anything else they've tried; Tyler thinks it's cheap and could hurt the brand's reputation long-term — do they do it?
**Recommendation:** Don't run it as "intentionally offensive." Board agreed the data is real but is measuring the wrong thing (engagement/short-term sales, not durable brand value or repeat-customer trust), and reputation damage isn't cleanly reversible the way a bad product test is. Consensus: bold, funny, attention-grabbing marketing is fine and even necessary — deliberately offensive for shock value is a different thing and not worth it. Real texture underneath: Jocko reframed this less as a tactics debate and more as a sign Tyler and his partner haven't actually agreed on what the brand's reputation is worth, i.e. a strategy disagreement wearing a tactics costume. Wozniak's tinkering instinct raised whether a small-scale test was reasonable; Housel and Jocko pushed back that reputation isn't safely A/B-testable the way a feature is — bad news travels fast, good news travels slow. Blakely drew a sharp line between "bold/weird" (which she's built her whole career on) and "offensive on purpose," and was the most open to edgy-but-not-cruel marketing of the five.
